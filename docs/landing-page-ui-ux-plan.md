# Landing Page UI/UX Plan

## Reference Patterns Reviewed

- Volt Athletics: leads with a direct performance platform promise, keeps `Get a Demo` and `Start Free Trial` prominent, then separates coaching, app, and athlete outcomes into clear follow-up sections.
- TrainHeroic: prioritizes a single coach trial path, uses a secondary browsing route, and keeps product value, audience proof, and resources in distinct bands.
- BridgeAthletic: starts with one solution selector and primary CTA, then moves through proof, platform builder value, delivery, education, testimonials, and security without forcing every point above the fold.

## UNLXCK Direction

- First viewport: one headline, one product promise, one primary CTA (`Start free beta`), one secondary CTA (`Log in`), and a compact product preview.
- Above-fold proof: replace dense cards and fake metrics with a product strip: Intake, Readiness, Camp Plan, Saved History.
- Segmentation: split content into four product proof bands: intake context, readiness constraints, structured camp plan, saved plan history.
- Process: keep the workflow to four steps: complete intake, run readiness check, generate fight camp, return between sessions.
- CTA rhythm: repeat the signup/login action only after the user has seen the workflow and value bands.
- Motto: use `Unlxck Your Potential` as a restrained brand line, not a generic hero eyebrow.

## Responsive Plan

- Mobile: hero content stacks first, product preview follows, proof strip becomes compact vertical rows, workflow cards stack, and the final CTA stacks.
- Tablet: hero and preview stack to avoid cramped two-column reading.
- Desktop: two-column hero with the product preview on the right and separate full-width sections below.

## Visual Rules

- Keep the premium black base, white typography, and restrained red action accents.
- Remove AI-generated boxer/fighter imagery for now. Use dark abstract texture, subtle red gradients, and product UI mockups until real athlete photos are available.
- Do not use fake social proof numbers. Proof must be product-based unless a metric is real.
- Avoid generic SaaS cards, soft wellness styling, and overloaded product chrome.
- Use short copy, strong hierarchy, and stable section spacing.
